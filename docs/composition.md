# Compose words and pictures

Plan the page count before final prose. Allocate cover, title, copyright, end
matter and story pages explicitly; a spread is two facing pages, not a synonym
for a beat. Several beats may fit a spread, and one beat may need more space.
Confirm the printer's eventual requirements during production.

For every spread, record its dominant action, emotional change and page-turn
reveal. Separate what the words convey from what the image conveys. The words
often carry motive, sound or thought; the image can carry setting, gesture,
movement and a second layer of humor. Use the
[scene and art brief](../templates/scene-art-brief.md).

## Short text over full illustrations

- Keep the causal chain and the protagonist's choice when shortening. Remove
  redundant description before removing the reason an action happens.
- Reserve the text area in the brief before generating or commissioning art.
  Keep essential text and faces out of the gutter and trim area.
- Use a clear typeface, generous spacing and stable contrast. A quiet patch of
  sky or a restrained text panel can help; inspect it at the intended reading
  size. Never shrink text simply to preserve a crowded layout.
- Fit translated text in the same planned zones, or adapt the layout. Keep
  lettering editable rather than baking a source language into the artwork.
- Check where the reader's eye enters and exits. Support the reading direction
  of the selected language; the current board does not promise automatic
  right-to-left book layout.

## Visual rhythm

Vary scale, framing and viewpoint to serve the action. A close-up can reveal
hesitation; a wider frame can show its consequence. Review two adjacent spreads
for accidental repetition, but do not change location or time merely to meet a
quota. Use quiet vignettes and larger images where their contrast helps.

Palette, light and weather may signal mood or time when consistent with the
scene. A background mini-story can reward rereading if it does not compete with
the central action. Frame-breaking effects must respect print safety zones.
Choose a final image that lets the ending settle; it need not always be the
widest image.

Inspect object support and contact: held objects need visible hands or another
clear support, feet need believable ground contact, and props must retain their
count and condition. Check each spread against the previous and next one for
costume, anatomy, direction, geography and light. An art brief is an instruction,
not evidence that the produced image follows it.
